7 checks before agreeing a company let.

A company let can work differently from a conventional tenancy. Whether you’re speaking to Maroogr or another operator, use these questions to understand the arrangement before you commit.

01

Who is granting—and receiving—the agreement?

Confirm the owner’s identity, title and authority to let. Check exactly which company or person will be your counterparty, and whether anyone else needs to consent. If you’re a leaseholder, read the superior lease; an owner’s assurance alone may not be enough.

02

Do your lender and freeholder agree?

Check your mortgage and any headlease or building rules against the actual proposed use. A generic “subletting allowed” statement may not cover a company let or short stays. Obtain any necessary permissions in writing before committing.

03

Does the insurance cover the real arrangement?

Tell the relevant buildings, contents and liability insurers who will occupy the property, how it will be used and who is responsible for each policy. Ask for written confirmation or an endorsement where needed. Do not assume an ordinary residential policy covers every arrangement.

04

Is the intended use clear and permitted?

Agree who may stay, for how long, whether rooms can be let separately and the maximum occupancy. Planning, housing licensing, safety and other requirements depend on the address and actual use. Ask which checks the operator has completed and which remain outstanding; obtain professional advice where needed.

05

What does the rent promise actually mean?

Put the rent, payment date, review arrangements, start date, duration and any conditions in writing. Understand which costs or deductions affect what you receive. A commercial description such as “guaranteed rent” is no substitute for reading the payment obligations and what happens if things go wrong.

06

Who handles each responsibility?

Create a clear split for utilities, council tax or rates, cleaning, maintenance, structural repairs, safety checks, damage, guest issues and emergencies. Include spending limits and how approvals work. Check what the operator will actually do—and what still needs your involvement.

07

How does the arrangement end?

Review the term, break rights, notice periods, early termination and what happens if consent is withdrawn or the arrangement cannot operate lawfully. Agree the handback condition, inspection process, treatment of damage and removal of any furnishings. Make sure you can recover possession as the contract promises.

Take your time. Read the complete agreement, not just a brochure or verbal summary. This checklist is general information, not legal advice. A solicitor and other appropriate property, tax and insurance professionals can advise on your particular property and circumstances.
